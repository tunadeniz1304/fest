# Robust Head-Pose & Gaze Estimation for a Dual-Angle (Frontal + Profile) Driver Monitoring System

## TL;DR
- **Replace solvePnP with a learned, appearance-based head-pose CNN.** The single most robust, practical choice for an unknown frontal-OR-profile camera on a Tesla T4 is **6DRepNet360** (or its lighter predecessor **6DRepNet**), which regresses a full 6D rotation representation and is explicitly designed for the full range of rotation — it does not collapse at profile angles the way your 6-point solvePnP does. **WHENet** (4.4M params, EfficientNet-B0) is the lightest full-range alternative.
- **Add gaze (L2CS-Net, or MediaPipe Iris) as a complementary "eyes-off-road" signal, but do not rely on it alone at profile angles.** Appearance-based gaze degrades only *moderately* with head yaw (the GazeOnce360 study measured error rising from 9.99° at 0–10° yaw to 11.07° at 60–90° yaw), but eye-region/iris methods break down when one eye is self-occluded in a true profile view.
- **Calibrate per-video against a "looking-at-road" baseline.** Because the camera angle is unknown, subtract a per-clip reference pose (the modal/forward head pose) instead of trusting absolute yaw = 0. This one change eliminates most of the false "looking to the side" detections you are seeing now.

## Key Findings

### Why your current pipeline fails at profile
MediaPipe Face Mesh + 6-point `solvePnP` is a sparse 2D→3D correspondence method. At profile it fails for two compounding reasons: (a) the BlazeFace detector and the landmark model lose accuracy as the face self-occludes, and (b) `solvePnP` with only 6 points is extremely sensitive to landmark noise and becomes geometrically ill-conditioned when the nose landmark migrates toward the silhouette edge — small pixel errors map to large yaw errors, which is exactly the spurious 50–80° yaw you observe. The fix is to move to **appearance-based** pose/gaze regressors that learn pose directly from the head crop and were trained on profile/wide-angle data, plus a per-video baseline so absolute calibration errors cancel.

### 1. Head-pose models — comparison
All models below are appearance-based (they regress pose from a face/head crop), so unlike 6-point solvePnP they do not depend on visible nose-to-eye geometry and degrade far more gracefully at profile. All run fully offline once weights are local, and all are PyTorch/Keras/ONNX models that run on a Tesla T4 (CUDA). Benchmark protocol throughout: trained on 300W-LP, tested on AFLW2000-3D and BIWI; full-range tested on CMU Panoptic.

**Most accurate across the widest yaw range.** Of the five models you named, only **WHENet** and **6DRepNet360** (the 360° successor to 6DRepNet) are purpose-built for the full range. 6DRepNet / 6DRepNet360 are the most accurate in the narrow-to-profile band — per *Expert Systems with Applications* (Algabri et al., 2024, S0957417423027951): *"the best, second-best, and third-best MAE on the Biwi dataset are 3.47, 3.48, and 3.52, respectively, which was achieved using the 6DRepNet, WHENet, and LSR methods."* For genuine rear/over-the-shoulder angles (beyond ±90°), WHENet's wrapped-loss design and 6DRepNet360's full-rotation training are the only options that hold up. **HopeNet, FSA-Net, and img2pose collapse on full-range data** (CMU-Panoptic MAE ≈ 15–17°), confirmed in the 6DRepNet/DirectMHP full-range evaluations where narrow-range models exceed ~15° MAE while 6DRepNet stays ~6.1°.

WHENet's own paper (Zhou & Gregson, BMVC 2020, arXiv:2005.10353) reports its full-range performance using a **wrapped** metric — *"we define the Absolute Wrapped Error (AWE) ... which properly handles wrapping of yaws"* — giving overall full-range MAWE = 7.66° (per-axis: yaw 8.51°, pitch 7.67°, roll 6.78° on combined CMU Panoptic + 300W-LP). Its narrow-range numbers (Table 1/2) are AFLW2000 MAE 5.42° (full model) / 4.83° (WHENet-V) and BIWI 3.81° / 3.48°. WHENet has **4.4 million parameters** (EfficientNet-B0 backbone); the paper reports only a preliminary embedded port *"approaching 60fps"* — no desktop-GPU latency figure is published, so benchmark it yourself on the T4.

### 2. Gaze estimation (eyes-off-road)
- **L2CS-Net** (Abdelrahman, Hempel, Khalifa & Al-Hamadi, arXiv:2203.03339): the strongest practical appearance-based gaze net. Per the paper: *"Our proposed model achieves state-of-the-art accuracy of 3.92° and 10.41° on MPIIGaze and Gaze360 datasets, respectively."* ResNet-50 backbone, regresses yaw and pitch with separate classification+regression heads. Pretrained `L2CSNet_gaze360.pkl` (Gaze360, full-range) and MPIIGaze weights from the official repo's Google Drive (github.com/Ahmednull/L2CS-Net); pip-installable (`l2cs`). Runs ~50 FPS on GPU (T4-compatible), fully offline; input crops 448×448 (or 224×224).
- **MediaPipe Iris**: lightweight, offline, CPU-capable, gives iris/eye landmarks but **no calibrated gaze vector** — you must derive direction geometrically, and it fails when an eye is occluded in profile.
- **Does gaze degrade as much as head-pose at profile?** It degrades *less abruptly* but is not immune. GazeOnce360 (Liu et al., arXiv:2603.17161, 2026), Table 9 "Gaze error (°) under different absolute head yaw angles," reports **9.99° at 0–10° yaw rising to 11.07° at 60–90° yaw**, concluding *"gaze error increases moderately with larger head rotations."* But the failure mode is occlusion: the GazeSymCAT paper (J. Computational Design & Eng., 2025) notes that *"under extreme head postures ... the eyes may be partially obscured ... leading to poor landmark recognition ... methods that rely solely on eye images may experience significant performance degradation."* A landmark-based gaze evaluation (arXiv:2603.24724) found the detector *"fails or produces unreliable results at extreme gaze and head pose angles,"* retaining only **73–82% of frames**. On Gaze360, error on rear-facing ("back") views is far worse than frontal (~19° back vs ~11° front; EyeShopper, built on Gaze360, reports MAE 19° on back-head images). **Conclusion: gaze is a good *complement* to head-pose, not a replacement, at profile.**

### 3. Camera-angle normalization for an unknown viewpoint
You cannot calibrate the jury's camera, so prefer self-calibrating, geometry-light methods:
- **Per-video baseline subtraction (strongly recommended).** Estimate the driver's dominant "looking forward at road" head pose over the clip (temporal mode/median of the predicted yaw) and measure deviations relative to *that* baseline, not absolute zero. The VTTI driver-gaze repo (github.com/VTTI/gaze-fixation-and-object-saliency) does exactly this: it stores auto-computed `front_pitch`/`front_yaw` correction values per video (computed by an `estimate_front_angle.py` step) and subtracts them. This directly cancels the constant offset that a side camera injects.
- **Image rectification / perspective correction.** Duke SMIIP's "Accurate Head Pose Estimation Using Image Rectification" shows correcting perspective distortion reduces head-pose MAE on BIWI; useful if you can estimate focal length.
- **Frontalization / reference-pose calibration.** Classic pose-normalization (Gaussian-process normalization, 3D point-distribution models) maps faces to a canonical frontal pose before estimation.
- **Extrinsics from face geometry.** Recover the camera-to-head rotation from the rigid face transform (or essential-matrix approach as in US Patent 11,068,704 "Head pose and distraction estimation") and subtract it. More complex and brittle — the baseline-subtraction approach achieves ~80% of the benefit for ~5% of the effort.

### 4. MediaPipe facial transformation matrix vs. custom solvePnP
MediaPipe's Face Landmarker / FaceGeometry module outputs a 4×4 **facial transformation matrix** described by Google as *"a rigid linear mapping from the canonical face metric landmark set into the runtime face metric landmark set in a way that minimizes a difference between the two."* Because it fits the **full dense mesh (468 metric landmarks)** to a learned canonical model — rather than 6 hand-picked points — it is inherently more numerically stable than your 6-point solvePnP, which is dominated by landmark noise and degenerates when the nose point crosses the face silhouette at profile. So at frontal-to-moderate angles the MediaPipe matrix should beat your custom solvePnP. **However**, it is still bounded by the underlying landmark model, which itself degrades at steep profile, and **no peer-reviewed head-to-head MAE comparison of the MediaPipe matrix vs. solvePnP at profile angles was found.** Treat the MediaPipe-matrix advantage as a strong architectural inference, not a measured result — and note it still inherits MediaPipe's profile-detection failures, which is why a dedicated head-pose CNN is the better primary path.

## Recommendations (staged, concrete)
1. **Profile-capable detection first.** Do **not** rely on MediaPipe BlazeFace for the side-camera case. Use RetinaFace or a YOLO head detector (WHENet ships with a YOLOv3 head detector; img2pose/6DRepNet demos use a RetinaFace-style detector). Cropping the head correctly is the largest single determinant of downstream accuracy.
2. **Primary head-pose = 6DRepNet** (pip-installable, RepVGG backbone, fast on T4, best narrow/profile accuracy). If the jury's "side" clips may include rear/over-the-shoulder views beyond ±90°, switch the primary to **6DRepNet360** or **WHENet** (use WHENet if T4 latency is tight given its 4.4M-param footprint).
3. **Per-video baseline calibration.** Over the clip (or a rolling window), take the modal forward head yaw as the "looking at road" reference and flag distraction as a deviation, e.g. |Δyaw| > 30–45° sustained for >~1.5 s. This is what makes the system angle-agnostic.
4. **Gaze cross-check.** Run **L2CS-Net** (or MediaPipe Iris for CPU-only) as a secondary eyes-off-road signal and fuse via OR/confidence logic. Down-weight gaze when the detected head yaw is extreme (eye likely occluded).
5. **MediaPipe as a frontal fallback only** — and if you keep it, use the **4×4 transformation matrix**, not 6-point solvePnP.
6. **Pre-stage all weights locally** before the no-internet run (Google Drive / cloud.ovgu.de links will be unreachable on the day).

**Thresholds that change the recommendation:**
- *Latency-constrained on T4 (many concurrent subsystems):* prefer WHENet (4.4M) or FSA-Net (1.2M) over ResNet-50/RepVGG-heavy models; quantize to FP16 (RepVGG re-parameterizes cleanly and is reported to run FP16 on T4 without accuracy loss).
- *"Side" videos genuinely beyond ±90° (looking back):* only WHENet / 6DRepNet360 will hold up; HopeNet/FSA-Net/img2pose are disqualified (CMU MAE 15–17°).
- *Detection is the bottleneck (face not found at profile):* invest in the detector before swapping the pose model — a missed crop dominates any MAE difference between pose models.

## Caveats
- **WHENet's full-range numbers use a wrapped error (MAWE), not plain MAE** — its 7.66° full-range figure is not directly comparable to other models' MAE.
- **No published benchmark measures any of these models on an actual A-pillar / side automotive camera.** BIWI / AFLW2000 / CMU-Panoptic numbers are proxies; real in-vehicle performance must be validated on your own footage.
- **The MediaPipe-matrix-beats-solvePnP claim is an architectural inference, not a measured benchmark** — no peer-reviewed head-to-head at profile was found.
- **6DRepNet parameter count (~41.4M, RepVGG-B1g2) reported by a community re-implementation** (jahongir7174/SixDRepNet, 792 img/s); the original repo emphasizes throughput over a stated MB size — confirm the exact `.pth` size locally.
- Reported FPS figures (L2CS-Net ~50 FPS, WHENet ~60 FPS embedded) come from different hardware than a T4 — measure your own latency.
- Several weights live on Google Drive / institutional clouds; mirror them before competition day.
